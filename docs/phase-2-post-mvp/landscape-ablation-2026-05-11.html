Headline finding

Landscape did real work. With it on, the run produced a measurably broader report (+16% words, +22% unique citations, +1 sub-question branch) from higher-quality sources (+43% tier-1, +44% peer-reviewed), at the cost of ~1.76× total tokens. The single biggest structural delta: LANDSCAPE spawned a [sparse] branch on medical/legal/finance risks — producing an entire section of the final report (Nature Medicine 2024 data-poisoning, PII leakage, healthcare GAN fairness inversion, FDA/EU regulatory status) that NO_LANDSCAPE never investigated.

Single seed. n=1 per arm, single query, single trial. Agent runs are stochastic — treat any specific magnitude as directional. Additionally, "landscape off" strips both the brief and the scouted-source pool; this experiment can't attribute observed differences to one half or the other.

Worth confirming — if unintended, it means LANDSCAPE's adversarial critique was weaker than designed, so fixing it would likely amplify LANDSCAPE's observed advantage.

What LANDSCAPE's terrain-mapping unlocked that the raw query didn't:

  • A dedicated [sparse] branch on high-stakes domains. This generated 15 citations and an entire final-report section. NO_LANDSCAPE never spawned a comparable branch — the decomposition from the raw query alone skipped straight past domain specificity.
  • A second, more targeted adversarial (model collapse is not a practical risk). LANDSCAPE fought on specific contested claims; NO_LANDSCAPE ran one broad steelman against the benefits narrative.
  • Evaluation classified as [contested] rather than [synthesis] — priors about disagreement rather than unity. The content overlaps, but LANDSCAPE's organization admits the controversy openly.
  • Sharper question phrasings (model collapse and diversity loss vs. data-quality risks including model collapse; bias amplification & mechanisms — the word "mechanisms" is a terrain-surfaced frame, not one the raw query implied).

What this tells us about the two hypotheses

H1 · does landscape change the final report?

Yes, observably.

  • +22% unique citations (55 vs. 45)
  • +43% peer-reviewed cited (13 vs. 9)
  • +43% tier-1 cited (10 vs. 7)
  • +16% report length (3,783 vs. 3,267 words)
  • Entire new section on high-stakes domains
  • Only 5/55 cited URLs overlap — the two reports source different evidence bodies
H2 · does landscape change the decomposition?

Yes, observably.

  • +1 analyst dispatch on a high-value [sparse] branch that yielded a full report section
  • +1 targeted adversarial analyst (2 vs. 1 — sharper, not broader)
  • Evaluation reclassified [synthesis] → [contested]
  • Sharper question phrasings ("mechanisms," "diversity loss")
  • Root sub-question spawn count: 7 vs. 5

The landscape brief appears to do two distinct jobs: (a) pre-identify contested topics so the researcher picks the right classification on the first try, and (b) surface sub-areas the researcher would miss from the raw query alone.

Caveats

  • Single seed. One trial per arm — can't separate signal from stochastic variation. Replicate 3–5× before treating magnitudes as reliable.
  • Brief + sources confounded. "Off" drops both; this pair can't say which half matters. A 3-arm follow-up (brief-only / sources-only / both-off) is cheap.
  • Judgment-tier routing anomaly. LANDSCAPE's adversarial dispatches bypassed GPT-5.4. If unintended, LANDSCAPE's edge is understated.
  • Scout search-budget ceiling (16/scout) binding in LANDSCAPE. 19 exhaustions. LANDSCAPE's evidence pool may have been capacity-limited — raising the cap could widen the gap further.
  • Broad multi-area query. A narrow lookup query would likely show a much smaller (or zero) landscape effect. Generalisation not demonstrated.

Next tests, in order of expected signal

  1. Same query, 3× replicates per arm — confirm the effect is bigger than inter-run noise.
  2. Three-arm ablation (brief-only / sources-only / both-off) — disentangle the two halves of landscape's contribution.
  3. Narrow/lookup query, LANDSCAPE vs. NO_LANDSCAPE — test whether LANDSCAPE still earns its 77% token overhead when terrain is well-known.
  4. LLM-as-judge pairwise grading of the two reports (blinded arm labels, rubric over coverage / specificity / calibration / terminology). Forced-choice is more reliable than eyeballing.
  5. Fix the judgment-tier routing regression and re-run LANDSCAPE — confirm the adversarial sections widen the gap.
